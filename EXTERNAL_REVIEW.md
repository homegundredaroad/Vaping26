# Vaping26 External Review Candidate

**Status:** external-review candidate, not a completed systematic review, clinical guideline or regulatory finding.

**Review objective:** obtain independent methodological criticism before Vaping26 advances from evidence infrastructure to conclusion-sensitive synthesis.

## What reviewers are being asked to assess

Vaping26 is an independent UK vaping and nicotine evidence observatory. Its private Research engine collects and validates source material; the public repository receives only allowlisted, manifest-verified outputs.

External review should focus on whether the project has a defensible route from discovery to synthesis, not on whether the current publication count is large.

Please challenge the project on five questions:

1. Is the comparator framework scientifically appropriate, particularly the separation of never-users, former smokers, continuing smokers and dual users?
2. Is the study-design taxonomy adequate for cessation, respiratory, cardiovascular, youth, exposure and laboratory evidence?
3. Which fields may be classified from bibliographic metadata, and which require full-text/source-grounded human review?
4. Where would Vaping26 duplicate existing living systematic reviews, and where could continuously updated structured evidence add value?
5. What minimum validation, risk-of-bias and reproducibility requirements should be met before any Vaping26 synthesis is considered scientifically useful?

## Current evidence state

The public evidence-card dataset is primarily a bibliographic/classification layer. It can include stable identifiers, source indexes, provisional study design, topic tags, integrity state, open-access state and review/synthesis-readiness flags.

Conclusion-sensitive fields such as population details, smoking history, comparator interpretation, outcomes, effect estimates, funding/conflicts and risk of bias are not treated as established merely because a record has been discovered.

A substantial proportion of records may still have provisional or unknown study-design/integrity metadata. That is a known limitation and a reason for external review, not something to conceal.

## Publication-date policy

Electronic publication date, issue/volume publication date and source-reported year can differ. A record assigned to a future issue is not automatically invalid when a primary bibliographic source confirms that it is already published online.

The Research engine should therefore retain, where available:

- source-reported year;
- electronic-publication date;
- issue/print publication date;
- retrieval date;
- source identifier used to verify the record;
- anomaly/review reason when dates conflict.

Implausible future dates should be quarantined. Legitimate ahead-of-print/forthcoming issue metadata should be preserved with an explicit explanation rather than silently rewritten.

## Evidence-synthesis boundary

Vaping26 must not infer a health conclusion from publication counts, topic counts, network position or machine classification.

Before a record can contribute to conclusion-sensitive synthesis, the intended workflow is:

1. identify the research question and eligible comparator;
2. verify the source and study identity;
3. confirm study design and population;
4. extract exposure/intervention, comparator and outcomes from source-grounded material;
5. record quantitative estimates and uncertainty where applicable;
6. assess methodological limitations and risk of bias;
7. record funding/conflicts and publication-integrity information;
8. require human review for conclusion-sensitive fields;
9. synthesise only records meeting the question-specific eligibility and review gate.

## AI and automation boundary

AI/ML may assist relevance screening and source-grounded candidate extraction, but it must not autonomously determine causation, safety, diagnosis, illegality, misconduct or criminality. Conclusion-sensitive extracted fields require provenance and human review before synthesis.

## Independence

Vaping26 should preserve independence from tobacco and vaping-industry funding where doing so is necessary to protect scientific credibility, publication eligibility and future institutional collaboration.

## Requested reviewer output

A useful review does not need to endorse Vaping26. Critical findings are preferred. Where possible, reviewers are invited to classify observations as:

- **critical** — could invalidate or materially distort an evidence conclusion;
- **major** — important methodological or reproducibility weakness;
- **minor** — useful improvement that does not alter the core scientific boundary;
- **question** — requires clarification or evidence from the Research engine.

Review comments and resulting changes should be recorded transparently so that the project can demonstrate how external criticism changed the methodology.
