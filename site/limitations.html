Limitations

What Vaping26 cannot claim.

Vaping26 is an evolving evidence observatory, not a substitute for clinical guidance, a completed systematic review, an enforcement authority or a laboratory exposure study.

Scientific evidence

  • Recent-first literature surveillance is not yet a complete historical systematic review.
  • Publication counts are not effect estimates or certainty ratings.
  • Observational findings can be confounded by smoking history, dual use, socioeconomic factors and other exposures.
  • Short-term biomarkers or emissions cannot automatically be translated into long-term disease risk.
  • Study design labels can be misclassified when bibliographic metadata are sparse and therefore remain provisional until review.

Prevalence

  • Survey mode, question wording, age coverage, weighting and definitions can change over time.
  • ONS adult and youth/school-based indicators should not be treated as one continuous series.
  • Small-area or subgroup estimates may have wide confidence intervals.

Products, retail and enforcement

  • Company-search candidates do not establish physical vape shops or wrongdoing.
  • A product not matched automatically to a regulatory record is not thereby illegal.
  • Enforcement activity reflects detection and enforcement priorities as well as underlying behaviour.
  • News and social-media material remain discovery/context streams unless corroborated.

AI and automated extraction

Automated extraction can be wrong. Vaping26 therefore keeps AI off by default for conclusion-forming tasks and requires source provenance, structured validation and human review for evidence fields that could influence synthesis.

Exposure context

Ambient air-quality or satellite information does not identify an individual vaping event. Such information may only be used as a background control where a specific study design justifies it.
